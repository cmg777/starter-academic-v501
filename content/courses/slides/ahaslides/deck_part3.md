## Presenter notes of the interactive slides

### C1 (core, about 1 min, after page 1)

**QR code: join**

Students join at ahaslides.com with code ZTR2I or by scanning the QR code. Names are required on join, so the leaderboard and the spinner wheels show real names. Keep this slide up while the room settles and watch the count rise.

### C2 (core, about 1.5 min, after page 1)

**In one word: which tool do you use for data analysis today?**

Warm-up, no right answer. Do not correct anyone yet. The same prompt returns at the end of class, so take a screenshot of this cloud for the comparison.

### C3 (core, about 1 min, after page 1)

**Before we start: how confident are you?**

Baseline confidence on a 1 to 5 scale. The same three statements return before the exit ticket, so the class can see its own change. Read the averages aloud without comment.

### C4 (core, about 0.5 min, after page 1)

**Questions? Ask them here at any time during class**

Q&A stays open on every slide (presentation setting: Q&A on all slides). Questions can be anonymous. Upvotes push a question to the top. Check the Q&A at the leaderboard break and at the end.

### C5 (opt, about 2 min, after page 50)

**Whose shoulders do you stand on? Name a researcher, book or course that shaped your work.**

A warm, personal opener after the two quotes. Read three answers aloud. Science is cumulative, and so is this course: the tools in this lecture were built by others for us to reuse.

### C6 (core, about 1 min, after page 50)

**Data science needs domain knowledge, coding and statistics. Which is your strongest?**

Most master students in development pick domain knowledge. The Venn diagram on the next slide puts data science where all three meet; the tools at the end of the lecture lower the coding bar.

### C7 (core, about 1 min, after page 52)

**Match each field to its focus**

Answer as listed. Spatial data science sits in the overlap on the slide: it treats location as information, not only as a label.

### C8 (core, about 1 min, after page 52)

**Guess the first law of geography**

Answer: near, distant. Waldo Tobler, 1970. The next slide shows the quote. It is the reason for spatial dependence: neighboring regions share markets, climate and policy, so their outcomes move together.

### C9 (opt, about 1.5 min, after page 53)

**Cold call: give an example of the first law of geography from your country**

Good examples: poverty clusters in neighboring districts, house prices, crime, disease spread, growth spilling over from a big city to its neighbors.

### C10 (core, about 1 min, after page 55)

**GeoDa is free point-and-click software. How many users did it have by 2020?**

Answer: C, about 360,000 users as of September 2020, shown at the start of the next video. GeoDa comes from the Center for Spatial Data Science (Luc Anselin).

### C11 (opt, about 1 min, after page 58)

**GeoDa requires programming skills.**

Answer: False. GeoDa is point-and-click, which makes it the easiest first step. The free video lectures of Prof. Anselin and the two-volume GeoDa workbook teach it from scratch.

### C12 (core, about 1 min, after page 60)

**Why is Google Earth Engine called a supercomputer for you?**

Answer: A. Earth Engine stores petabytes of satellite images and runs your code in the cloud; your laptop only sends the script and receives the result. The video that follows shows it.

Unscored. A reasonable picture: GeoDa and Excel easy but weak with images; Earth Engine strongest with images and moderate to start; R and Python flexible, harder to start, strong once learned. There is no single best tool; it depends on the question.

### C17 (core, about 8 min, after page 71)

**Colab stop: run the Bolivia notebook through Section 4. Which municipality has the LOWEST development index (imds)?**

The coding stop, about eight minutes. Students open https://colab.research.google.com/github/quarcs-lab/project2021o-notebook/blob/main/notebookColab.ipynb (the Link to the tutorial on the slide), run the setup cells, load the data and run the table sorted by imds in Section 4. The last row is Poroma (35.7), the least developed of the 339 municipalities; La Paz is first (80.2). Students who finish early can open the maps in Section 5 and look for clusters of low imds: the first law of geography again.
