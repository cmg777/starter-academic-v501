## 11 — Content

**Title:** DiD is a double difference: 36.20 minus 10.88 equals 25.32

**Image page:** 10 of 33

**Notes:** The treated runner sped up by 36.20 units; the comparison runner by 10.88. The coaching effect is the extra 25.32 units only the coached runner gained. Rounding note: 25.32 is the arithmetic on means rounded to two decimals; with unrounded means the DiD is 25.315 (trend 10.886), exactly the regression coefficient on the coming slides. The naive 36.20 overstates 25.32 by 43%.

## 12 — Content

**Title:** The counterfactual: where treated schools would have landed without tutoring

**Image page:** 11 of 33

**Notes:** The counterfactual is the treated pre-period level (60.17) plus the comparison group's change (10.88) = 71.05: where treated schools would be if they had only drifted with the trend. The actual post mean is 96.37; the gap of 25.32 is the program's effect. Parallel trends does not require equal levels (treated schools start lower), only equal trends.

## 13 — Content

**Title:** DiD identifies the ATT, not the ATE, under parallel trends

**Image page:** 12 of 33

**Notes:** State the estimand explicitly. DiD recovers the ATT, the effect on the treated, not the ATE. The two diverge when treatment effects vary with selection into treatment. Parallel trends is the identifying assumption, and it concerns an unobserved counterfactual, so no test can prove it. SUTVA (no spillovers between schools) is the other load-bearing assumption.

---

## 14 — Content

**Title:** A treated×post interaction recovers the same 25.32, and every coefficient is a group mean

**Image page:** 13 of 33

**Notes:** The manual double difference is exactly an OLS regression with a treated dummy, a post dummy and their interaction. Every coefficient is a group mean or a difference of group means; txp = 25.315 is the ATT. HC1 is heteroskedasticity-robust but treats each school's two observations as independent; the next model clusters by school.

## 23 — Content

**Title:** Inference choices barely move the needle when the signal is this strong

**Image page:** 19 of 33

**Notes:** The answer to the vote: C. CRV3 re-estimates the model leaving out one school at a time; it corrects CRV1's tendency to understate uncertainty when clusters are few or influential. ## 37 — Content

**Title:** Does precision make this causal? No — the assumptions still carry it

**Image page:** 32 of 33

**Notes:** Steelman, do not strawman. The event study probes but cannot confirm parallel trends; it only checks the pre-period. SUTVA (no spillovers) is assumed, not tested. With staggered real-world timing and effects that vary across adoption cohorts, the standard TWFE estimator can be badly biased (Goodman-Bacon; Callaway–Sant'Anna; Gardner). This deck evaluates a method on clean data, not a substantive claim about tutoring.
