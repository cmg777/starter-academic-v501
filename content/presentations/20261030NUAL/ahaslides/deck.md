## Presenter notes

**N0** (qr_code, slide 2): Join slide. The audience scans the code or types the join code at ahaslides.com. No names are asked. Keep it up while people settle, then move to the agenda.

**N1** (word_cloud, slide 4): Warm-up, no right answer. Read the three biggest words aloud. Words such as writing, coding or searching set up the point of Section 1: AI makes production cheap.

**N2** (poll, slide 7): Ask right after the trade-off chart. The third option (cheap to make, rarely verified) is the danger zone: cheap to make, expensive to confirm. Honest answers are the point, so stress that the poll is anonymous. Whatever its share, the rest of the talk is about moving that bar to the second option.

**N3** (scale, slide 25): Closes the learning section. Read both averages aloud. A gap between the two (willing to try, less sure about verifying) is the bridge to Section 3, where GitHub makes verification concrete.

**N4** (open_ended_survey, slide 38): Take-away. Give a minute, then read three or four answers. Answers are anonymous.

**N5** (q&a, slide 39): Q&A is open on all slides, so questions asked during the talk are already here. Upvotes push a question to the top. Answer the top three, then close on the Thank You slide.
