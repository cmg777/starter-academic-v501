# Infographic instructions — `python_bridge_impact`

Chalkboard-style storyboard infographic for *Evaluating the Impact of Infrastructure: A Beginner's
Guide to Difference-in-Differences with the Jamuna Bridge*.

**Story spine:** A bridge made a poor region lose its factories — and that turned out to be good
news, but only because someone also counted the people.

**Palette (infographic-specific, not the site palette):**
navy `#0e1545` · chalk white `#f0ece2` · steel blue `#8bb8e0` · warm orange `#e8956a` ·
teal `#00d4c8` · muted gray `#b0a89a`

---

## A. Image prompt

> A single wide landscape infographic rendered as an illustrated chalkboard, drawn as if a patient
> teacher filled a large slate wall over the course of one long lecture. The background is a deep
> matte navy, colour `#0e1545`, with the faint uneven texture of slate that has been written on and
> wiped many times — soft ghosting, a few dusty smears near the edges, never so heavy that it
> competes with the drawing. Everything on the board is rendered in chalk: slightly grainy strokes,
> visible pressure variation, the occasional line that overshoots its corner the way real hand-drawn
> chalk does. The dominant chalk colour is a warm off-white, `#f0ece2`. Accents are drawn in exactly
> three further chalk colours and no others: a soft steel blue `#8bb8e0`, a warm orange `#e8956a`,
> and a bright teal `#00d4c8`. A muted gray `#b0a89a` is used only for secondary annotation and
> hatching. There is no photographic element anywhere, no gradient mesh, no glow, no drop shadow, no
> gloss — the whole surface should read as matte chalk on matte slate.
>
> The board is divided into six panels arranged in a three-across, two-down grid. The panels are
> separated by thin hand-drawn chalk rules in muted gray, slightly wobbly, drawn freehand rather
> than ruled. Each panel carries a small hand-lettered number in its top-left corner, one through
> six, circled in chalk. Each panel has a short hand-lettered title in the warm off-white, in a
> confident sans-serif chalk hand, roughly twice the size of the body annotation.
>
> Panel one, top-left, titled "Two rivers, one bridge". A simplified map of a river delta drawn in
> steel-blue chalk: two broad rivers cutting a landmass into three parts, drawn as flowing double
> lines with a suggestion of current. The left region is labelled in chalk "Jamuna hinterland", the
> right "Padma hinterland", and the middle "the capital". Across the left river a small bridge is
> drawn in bright teal, with a tiny chalk truck crossing it and short motion lines behind. Across
> the right river there is no bridge — instead a dashed teal outline of a bridge that was never
> built, with a small hand-lettered note beside it reading "not begun until 2015". At the bottom of
> the panel, a chalk arrow curls from the left region toward the capital with the annotation "20
> hours becomes 6".
>
> Panel two, top-centre, titled "Three theories, one problem". Three chalk speech bubbles fan out
> from a small drawn question mark. The first bubble, in steel blue, reads "the region revives".
> The second, in warm orange, reads "the region is hollowed out". The third, in teal, reads "the
> region specialises". Beneath the second and third bubbles, a bracket drawn in muted gray joins
> them, with the hand-lettered annotation "these two predict the same thing about factories".
> A small chalk factory with a chimney sits under the bracket, with a downward arrow through it in
> orange, drawn identically under both bubbles to make the ambiguity visual.
>
> Panel three, top-right, titled "Four numbers, two subtractions". A small hand-drawn line chart on
> a chalk axis. Two solid lines rise from left to right: an orange one climbing steeply and a
> steel-blue one climbing gently. A third line, drawn in teal and dashed, extends from the orange
> line's starting point at the blue line's gentler slope. A vertical chalk bracket spans the gap
> between where the orange line ends and where the teal dashed line ends, annotated "the effect".
> A vertical dotted chalk line crosses the whole chart with the small label "bridge opens". In the
> lower corner, in small chalk hand: "whatever moved both regions cancels out".
>
> Panel four, bottom-left, titled "The tell". This panel is built entirely as a side-by-side
> comparison, drawn as two chalk columns separated by a vertical rule. The left column is headed
> "if the region is emptying" and shows, in warm orange, a downward arrow beside a small chalk
> factory and a second downward arrow beside a cluster of small chalk human figures. The right
> column is headed "if the region is specialising" and shows, in orange, the same downward arrow
> beside the same factory, but in teal an upward arrow beside the same cluster of figures. A large
> hand-drawn chalk magnifying glass hovers over the figures in both columns, connecting them, with
> the annotation "the factories look identical — count the people instead".
>
> Panel five, bottom-centre, titled "It was not near the bridge". A horizontal chalk band running
> the width of the panel, divided into three segments labelled "nearest", "middle" and "farthest",
> with a small teal bridge icon at the left end. Above each segment, a vertical chalk bar shows the
> gain: short and steel blue over "nearest", slightly taller over "middle", and dramatically taller
> and drawn in bright teal over "farthest". A hand-drawn chalk arrow sweeps from the bridge icon
> along the band toward the tall teal bar, with the annotation "a small discount on a huge cost
> beats a big discount on a small one".
>
> Panel six, bottom-right, titled "What survived". A chalk balance scale, hand-drawn and slightly
> asymmetric. On the left pan, a small factory with a downward orange arrow. On the right pan, a
> cluster of human figures with an upward teal arrow, drawn noticeably larger and tipping the scale
> that way. Beneath the scale, three short hand-lettered lines, each preceded by a small chalk
> tick: "backwash rejected", "the region specialised", and "122 of 122 coefficients reproduced".
>
> Three callouts, and only three, are rendered much larger than any other text on the board: they
> should read as the numbers a viewer takes away from across a room. Each is hand-lettered in
> chalk inside a rough hand-drawn box. The first sits in panel four and reads "+5.9%" in bright
> teal with the small caption beneath it "population density, long run". The second sits in panel
> four's left half and reads "−1.2 pp" in warm orange with the caption "manufacturing share". The
> third sits in panel five and reads "+26.5%" in bright teal with the caption "rice yield, farthest
> from the bridge". No other number on the board is rendered at that scale.
>
> Across the very top of the board, above the panel grid, a single hand-lettered headline in large
> warm off-white chalk: "A region can lose its factories and still be better off". Along the very
> bottom edge, in small muted gray chalk, a single line: "Jamuna Bridge, Bangladesh, 1998 ·
> difference-in-differences · 248 upazilas".
>
> Composition should feel deliberately hand-made and a little imperfect — panels not perfectly
> aligned, lettering baselines drifting slightly, a smudge or two where a hand rested. It should
> look like the residue of a very good lecture, not like a corporate slide.

## C. Condensed prompt (under 250 words)

> A wide chalkboard infographic on deep navy slate `#0e1545`, drawn entirely in matte chalk: warm
> off-white `#f0ece2` for line work and lettering, with accents in steel blue `#8bb8e0`, warm
> orange `#e8956a` and bright teal `#00d4c8`, and muted gray `#b0a89a` for secondary annotation.
> Six hand-drawn panels in a 3×2 grid, separated by wobbly freehand chalk rules, each numbered in a
> chalk circle and hand-lettered with a short title.
>
> Panel 1 "Two rivers, one bridge": a simplified delta map, one river bridged in teal with a tiny
> truck crossing, the other showing a dashed outline of a bridge never built.
> Panel 2 "Three theories, one problem": three speech bubbles, two of them bracketed together with
> identical downward arrows through a chalk factory.
> Panel 3 "Four numbers, two subtractions": two rising chalk lines plus a dashed teal counterfactual,
> with a bracket marking the gap.
> Panel 4 "The tell": a two-column comparison — factories fall in both, but people fall on the left
> and rise on the right, with a chalk magnifying glass over the figures.
> Panel 5 "It was not near the bridge": three bars along a distance band, tallest at the far end.
> Panel 6 "What survived": a tipping chalk balance scale with three ticked conclusions.
>
> Exactly three oversized hand-lettered numbers in rough chalk boxes: "+5.9%" (teal), "−1.2 pp"
> (orange), "+26.5%" (teal). Headline across the top: "A region can lose its factories and still be
> better off". Deliberately hand-made, slightly imperfect, matte throughout.
