# Data dictionary - Bayesian Spatial Synthetic Control in Python: California's Proposition 99

Companion data for a Python tutorial that fits three nested synthetic-control estimators to California's Proposition&nbsp;99: the classical simplex of Abadie, Diamond &amp; Hainmueller (2010), a Bayesian horseshoe prior on unconstrained weights, and the Bayesian spatial (SAR) model of Sakaguchi &amp; Tagawa (2026). The outcome panel is the same <strong>real</strong> tobacco data used throughout the synthetic-control literature: per-capita cigarette sales (<code>cigsale</code>) and real retail price (<code>retprice</code>) for 39 US states over 1970&ndash;2000, as distributed in the <code>scspill</code> package. California is the one treated unit; the other 38 states form the donor pool.


Companion data for the post <https://carlos-mendez.org/post/python_sc_bayes_spatial/>. Each dataset ships as a labeled Stata `.dta` (v118) plus its source file. Generated by `build_data_dictionary.py`.

## Known limitations & caveats

- <strong>Narrow predictor set.</strong> The shipped panel carries only <code>cigsale</code> and <code>retprice</code> — not the log income, youth-share or beer-sales predictors Abadie et al. (2010) matched on. This is the main reason the classical ATT here (≈ −18.4) is smaller in magnitude than the ≈ −27 usually quoted from the original paper.
- <strong>Treatment timing is 1988, not 1989.</strong> Proposition 99 passed in November 1988 and the tax took effect on 1 January 1989. Abadie et al. and mlsynth's own example treat 1989 as the first treated year; the scspill replication convention used here codes 1988. Neither is wrong, but they cannot be mixed — comparisons against other sources should check which convention that source used.
- <strong>Contiguity is a modelling choice, not a measurement.</strong> The edge list is rook contiguity from a 2024 shapefile applied to a 1970–2000 panel. Borders did not move, but 'neighbour' could reasonably have meant trade intensity, commuting flows or inverse distance instead — and the estimated spillover intensity would differ. The post's section 17 discusses when each is appropriate.
- <strong>Eleven states are absent.</strong> Alaska, Arizona, Florida, Hawaii, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, New Jersey, New York, Oregon and Washington are excluded from the donor pool for having run their own large tobacco-control programmes. Two of them — Oregon and Arizona — border California. Their exclusion is why Nevada is California's <em>only</em> contiguous donor, and therefore why the spatial parameter is identified off a single channel.
- <strong>The weights are unnormalised here.</strong> Both files store raw 0/1 contiguity. Row-normalisation happens inside the estimator, not in the data.
